Your 7-Day Bone-Building Meal Plan: Simple Recipes for Stronger Bones

April 6, 2026 · By Dr. Lisa Pocius

When I first started eating for my bones after my diagnosis, the biggest challenge wasn't knowledge. It was figuring out what to actually make for dinner on a Tuesday night. I knew calcium mattered, and vitamin D, and protein. But translating that into a week of real meals? That took some work.

So I put together the meal plan I wish I'd had from the beginning. Every meal here is simple, made from ingredients you can find at any grocery store, and designed to deliver the nutrients your bones need most: calcium, vitamin D, protein, magnesium, and vitamin K.

You don't have to follow this plan perfectly. Swap meals between days, repeat your favorites, adjust portion sizes. The goal is consistency over perfection. If you eat this way most of the time, your bones will notice.

How to Use This Plan

Each day includes breakfast, lunch, dinner, and one snack. I've included a nutrient callout for every meal so you can see roughly how much calcium and protein you're getting. These are estimates, not exact measurements. Your bones don't need you to weigh every ounce of yogurt.

At the bottom of this page, you'll find a consolidated grocery list organized by section. Hit the "Print this plan" button to take it with you to the store.

Day 1: Monday

Breakfast
Greek Yogurt Parfait with Berries and Almonds
1 cup plain Greek yogurt, topped with 1/2 cup mixed berries, 2 tbsp sliced almonds, and 1 tbsp chia seeds. Drizzle with honey if you like.
Calcium: ~300mgProtein: 22gMagnesium: 60mg
Lunch
Salmon and Kale Salad
4 oz baked or canned salmon over 2 cups chopped kale, massaged with lemon and olive oil. Add cherry tomatoes, red onion, and a sprinkle of pumpkin seeds.
Calcium: ~250mgProtein: 30gVitamin D: ~570 IU
Dinner
Chicken Stir-Fry with Bok Choy and Brown Rice
5 oz chicken breast, sliced and sauteed with 2 cups bok choy, broccoli florets, garlic, and a splash of soy sauce. Serve over 3/4 cup brown rice.
Calcium: ~200mgProtein: 38gVitamin K: 85mcg
Snack
Handful of Almonds (1 oz) and a Clementine
Calcium: ~75mgProtein: 6gMagnesium: 75mg
Daily Bone Boost Tip

Take a 15-minute walk after lunch. Even brief sun exposure helps your body produce vitamin D, and the weight-bearing movement supports your bones.

Day 2: Tuesday

Breakfast
Overnight Oats with Fortified Milk
1/2 cup rolled oats soaked overnight in 1 cup fortified almond or dairy milk, with 1 tbsp almond butter, sliced banana, and a pinch of cinnamon.
Calcium: ~350mgProtein: 14gMagnesium: 55mg
Lunch
White Bean and Kale Soup
Hearty soup with 1 cup white beans, chopped kale, diced carrots, celery, garlic, and chicken or vegetable broth. Serve with a slice of whole grain bread.
Calcium: ~280mgProtein: 18gVitamin K: 110mcg
Dinner
Baked Salmon with Steamed Broccoli and Quinoa
5 oz salmon fillet baked with lemon and herbs. Serve alongside 1.5 cups steamed broccoli and 3/4 cup cooked quinoa.
Calcium: ~150mgProtein: 42gVitamin D: ~570 IU
Snack
Cheese and Whole Grain Crackers
1 oz aged cheddar or Gouda with 4-5 whole grain crackers.
Calcium: ~200mgProtein: 8gVitamin K2: 15mcg
Daily Bone Boost Tip

Shake your fortified plant milk carton well before pouring. The added calcium tends to settle at the bottom, so a good shake makes sure you actually get the nutrients listed on the label.

Day 3: Wednesday

Breakfast
Two Eggs with Sauteed Greens and Toast
2 eggs (any style) served over sauteed collard greens or kale. Side of whole grain toast with a thin spread of butter.
Calcium: ~230mgProtein: 20gVitamin D: ~80 IU
Lunch
Sardines on Toast with Arugula
One 3 oz can of sardines (with bones) on whole grain toast. Top with arugula, a squeeze of lemon, and a drizzle of olive oil.
Calcium: ~325mgProtein: 22gVitamin D: ~45 IU
Dinner
Tofu and Vegetable Stir-Fry
6 oz calcium-set tofu, cubed and pan-fried, with bok choy, bell peppers, snap peas, and sesame seeds. Serve over brown rice with a drizzle of sesame oil.
Calcium: ~350mgProtein: 24gMagnesium: 80mg
Snack
Apple Slices with Almond Butter
1 medium apple, sliced, with 2 tbsp almond butter.
Calcium: ~55mgProtein: 7gMagnesium: 50mg
Daily Bone Boost Tip

I'll be honest: sardines took some getting used to. But on toast with lemon and arugula, they're genuinely good. One little can delivers calcium, vitamin D, and omega-3s all at once.

Day 4: Thursday

Breakfast
Smoothie with Fortified Milk, Banana, and Chia Seeds
Blend 1 cup fortified milk (dairy or plant-based), 1 banana, 1 tbsp chia seeds, 1/2 cup frozen berries, and a handful of baby kale or spinach.
Calcium: ~350mgProtein: 12gMagnesium: 65mg
Lunch
Turkey and Avocado Wrap with Cheese
Whole grain wrap with 3 oz sliced turkey, 1 oz Swiss cheese, sliced avocado, lettuce, and tomato. Side of baby carrots.
Calcium: ~250mgProtein: 28gVitamin K: 25mcg
Dinner
Grilled Chicken with Collard Greens and Sweet Potato
5 oz grilled chicken breast, 1 cup sauteed collard greens with garlic, and 1 medium baked sweet potato.
Calcium: ~300mgProtein: 40gVitamin K: 530mcg
Snack
Yogurt with Walnuts
3/4 cup plain yogurt with 2 tbsp chopped walnuts and a drizzle of honey.
Calcium: ~230mgProtein: 12gMagnesium: 30mg
Daily Bone Boost Tip

Your body can only absorb about 500mg of calcium at a time. That's why spreading calcium-rich foods throughout the day (rather than loading up at one meal) gives your bones the best chance to use what you eat.

Day 5: Friday

Breakfast
Cottage Cheese Bowl with Fruit and Seeds
1 cup cottage cheese topped with sliced peaches or pineapple, 1 tbsp pumpkin seeds, and a sprinkle of ground flaxseed.
Calcium: ~190mgProtein: 28gMagnesium: 45mg
Lunch
Canned Salmon Salad on Greens
One 3 oz can of salmon (with bones), flaked and mixed with a bit of mayo and lemon. Serve over mixed greens with cucumber, red onion, and a sprinkle of sesame seeds.
Calcium: ~250mgProtein: 24gVitamin D: ~450 IU
Dinner
Lentil Soup with a Side of Cheese Toast
Bowl of lentil soup (lentils, carrots, celery, onion, cumin, broth). Serve with a slice of whole grain bread topped with 1 oz melted cheddar.
Calcium: ~260mgProtein: 26gMagnesium: 70mg
Snack
Fortified Orange Juice and a Handful of Brazil Nuts
8 oz calcium-fortified orange juice and 3 Brazil nuts.
Calcium: ~350mgProtein: 4gMagnesium: 55mg
Daily Bone Boost Tip

Try not to take calcium-rich foods or supplements at the same time as your coffee. Caffeine can increase the amount of calcium lost through urine. Space them out by at least 30 minutes.

Day 6: Saturday

Breakfast
Veggie Omelet with Cheese
3-egg omelet with sauteed bell peppers, onion, and 1 oz shredded cheddar. Side of whole grain toast.
Calcium: ~280mgProtein: 26gVitamin D: ~120 IU
Lunch
Tuna Melt on Whole Grain Bread
One can of tuna mixed with a little mayo, topped with 1 oz Swiss cheese and broiled on whole grain bread. Side of mixed green salad with olive oil dressing.
Calcium: ~280mgProtein: 34gVitamin D: ~150 IU
Dinner
White Bean and Chicken Sausage Skillet with Greens
1 cup white beans sauteed with sliced chicken sausage, diced tomatoes, garlic, and 2 cups of kale. Serve with crusty bread.
Calcium: ~300mgProtein: 32gVitamin K: 140mcg
Snack
Dark Chocolate Square and Mixed Nuts
1 oz dark chocolate (70%+) and a small handful of almonds and cashews.
Calcium: ~60mgProtein: 5gMagnesium: 65mg
Daily Bone Boost Tip

Weekend mornings are great for a slightly longer walk or some gentle strength exercises. Even 20 minutes of body-weight exercises (squats, wall push-ups, heel raises) sends bone-building signals to your skeleton.

Day 7: Sunday

Breakfast
Whole Grain Pancakes with Yogurt and Berries
2 whole grain pancakes (use fortified milk in the batter). Top with 1/2 cup Greek yogurt and fresh berries instead of syrup.
Calcium: ~280mgProtein: 18gMagnesium: 40mg
Lunch
Broccoli Cheddar Soup with Whole Grain Roll
Homemade or store-bought broccoli cheddar soup (made with real cheese and milk). Serve with a whole grain roll.
Calcium: ~320mgProtein: 16gVitamin K: 110mcg
Dinner
Baked Cod with Roasted Vegetables and Quinoa
5 oz baked cod with roasted broccoli, sweet potato, and zucchini. Serve with 3/4 cup quinoa. Squeeze of lemon on top.
Calcium: ~130mgProtein: 36gMagnesium: 85mg
Snack
Glass of Fortified Milk with a Banana
8 oz fortified milk (dairy or plant-based) and 1 medium banana.
Calcium: ~300mgProtein: 9gMagnesium: 40mg
Daily Bone Boost Tip

Use Sunday to prep a few things for the week ahead. Cook a batch of quinoa, wash your greens, and hard-boil some eggs. A little prep now means bone-healthy meals come together faster all week.

A Few Notes on This Plan

This meal plan provides roughly 1,000 to 1,200mg of calcium per day from food, plus meaningful amounts of vitamin D, protein, magnesium, and vitamin K. If you're supplementing calcium (which many of us are), you may need less from supplements on days when you eat this way.

Remember what I mentioned in my book: your body can only absorb about 500mg of calcium at once, so spreading your intake throughout the day matters more than the total number. That's why every meal here includes some calcium, rather than packing it all into one big dairy-heavy dinner.

If you're lactose intolerant or prefer plant-based options, fortified almond, soy, or oat milk can replace dairy in any of these meals. Just check labels, because calcium content varies dramatically between brands.

What Comes Next

A meal plan is a great start, but it works best alongside the other pillars of bone health. If you're new here, my Bone-Health Grocery Guide goes deeper into which foods deliver which nutrients and why. And remember: nutrition is just one piece. Weight-bearing exercise, adequate vitamin D (from sunshine and supplements if needed), and working with your healthcare team all play a role.

You don't need to be perfect. You need to be consistent. Start with the meals that sound good to you, and build from there. Your bones are rebuilding themselves every day. What you put on your plate is the raw material they have to work with.

Make it count.
