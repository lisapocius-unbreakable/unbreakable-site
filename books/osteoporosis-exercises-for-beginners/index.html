Osteoporosis Exercises for Beginners

A safe, illustrated 12-week program you can do at home.

Major bone health organizations recommend strength, balance, and posture exercises for people with osteoporosis. After a diagnosis, though, many people aren't sure which exercises fit them or how to begin.

I have osteoporosis myself, including four fractures in my spine. This program starts supported and adds challenge a little at a time, using a sturdy chair, a wall, and a kitchen counter at home.

What's inside

  • More than 40 illustrated exercises for strength, posture, balance, and safe impact, each with start and finish pictures and plain step-by-step directions
  • The everyday movements to modify, and the safer way to do each one
  • A home setup that uses a chair, a wall, and a kitchen counter
  • A no-floor version of every floor exercise
  • A 12-week plan built around two or three strength days a week, using only exercises taught in the book
  • Ways to adapt the program if you've had a spine fracture, have sore joints, or feel nervous about your balance
  • Printable logs and trackers so you can see your progress between scans

How the book is organized

Part 1, Start Safely, covers the movements to avoid before you begin and how to set up your space and find your starting point. Part 2 is the illustrated exercise library. Part 3 turns those exercises into your own routine and the 12-week program, with ways to adapt it and keep going afterward. Part 4 holds the printable daily log, the 12-week progress tracker, an equipment substitution chart, and a quick safety reference.

This book is general education, not medical advice. Before you start, and any time something hurts, stop and check with your doctor or physical therapist.

A companion to my first book

This book is all about movement. It doesn't cover calcium, vitamin D, medications, or DEXA and FRAX results. Those are in Osteoporosis: The Book I Wish I'd Had When I Was Diagnosed, which also comes as an enlarged-type hardcover and an audiobook.
